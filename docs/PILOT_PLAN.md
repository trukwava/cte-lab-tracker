# Two-week single-lab shadow pilot

**Purpose:** Determine whether the workflow reduces inventory ambiguity and administrative effort without encouraging unverified safety or procurement decisions. A real school/customer has not been interviewed or committed to participate.

## Five discovery questions

1. During a live lesson, how do you currently locate an asset and verify that it is available, and what interrupts that task?
2. Who decides that an inspected or repaired item can return to use, and what evidence or note do they need to retain?
3. How are borrower codes assigned, when is a class-kit checkout preferable, and what borrower information must be excluded?
4. How is maintenance due determined today (time, usage, local policy), and which source is authoritative?
5. What are the exact handoff steps between a missing tool, a replacement request and actual school purchasing approval?

## Baseline and shadow sequence

**Days 1–2:** With consenting authorized staff and no student data, observe 10 routine tool checkouts and returns using existing practice. Time from selecting asset to completed recorded issue (seconds), record count of lookup interruptions, outstanding missing items and completion of scheduled maintenance notes. Measure the administrative number of entries per transaction; do not infer time savings from synthetic demonstration.

**Days 3–7:** Run the tracker in *shadow mode* alongside authoritative existing records, using synthetic or appropriately cleared de-identified information. No live change of safety, purchasing or official custody decisions. Record mismatches and staff corrections; reconcile daily.

**Days 8–10:** Repeat measured tasks, compare distributions (median, range, outliers), log role confusion, stale-screen rejections and mobile usability. Review defects and audit trail with the instructor before deciding whether to continue.

## Hypothesized success measures (not observed results)

- ≥90% of sampled issues/returns recorded without facilitator intervention.
- Zero duplicate active loans or blocked-asset checkouts.
- Every reported defect retains a block until documented instructor action.
- Median end-to-end checkout time does not exceed baseline and shows a credible downward trend after practice.
- At least 90% of maintenance due items have a documented reviewed outcome or explicit overdue reason.
- Staff can describe every manual entry and would choose to use the tracker again.

**Stop/failure criteria:** Any issued loan duplication, unauthorized release, exposure of borrower details to a signed-out viewer, missing audit attribution, ambiguous safety language that could affect a real decision, significant repeated data-entry burden, or data-loss incident. Restore authoritative workflow and investigate before expanding scope.

**Next scope decision:** Only after a successful shadow trial, confirm actual user/administration demand, buyer and integration constraints. Prioritize real identity, import mapping, testable accessibility and operational reliability before consumables or district expansion. Buyer, pricing, adoption and time-savings remain unvalidated hypotheses.
